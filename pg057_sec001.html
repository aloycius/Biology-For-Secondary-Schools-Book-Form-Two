Digestion in a ruminant starts in the

mouth when plant material is eaten and

partly chewed by the teeth. The partly

chewed food is mixed with saliva

from the salivary glands and then

rolled into a bolus by the tongue. This

partly chewed and rolled food is then

swallowed through the oesophagus.

It then enters the rumen where it is

temporarily stored. The food stored in

the rumen is coarse and greenish. The

rumen is the largest part of the three

chambers of the ruminant stomach.

Hence, it can store a large amount of

food at a time. In the rumen, there are

bacteria that act upon the swallowed

food and digest the cellulose contained

in the plant materials. Figure 2.8 shows

the internal structure of the ruminant’s

digestive system.
