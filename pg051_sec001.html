The ileum is the final part of the small intestine. The process of digestion ends in the ileum. When chyme enters the ileum, it stimulates the intestinal wall of the ileum to secrete an intestinal juice known as succus entericus. This contains digestive enzymes that finalise the digestion of proteins, carbohydrates and lipids (fats and oils). The digestive enzymes found in the ileum include the following:

Lipase

This enzyme catalyses the conversion of the remaining fats and oils into fatty acid and glycerol.

Maltase

It catalyses the conversion of maltose into glucose.

Sucrase

It catalyses the conversion of sucrose into glucose and fructose.

Lactase

It catalyses the conversion of lactose into glucose and galactose.

Peptidase

It catalyses the conversion of the remaining peptides into amino acids.
